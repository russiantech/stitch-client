The Problem: A ₦3.2 Trillion Market Running on Memory

Nigeria's laundry and fashion sector serves millions of working-class Nigerians weekly, yet operates almost entirely in the informal economy. While fintech digitized payments and e-commerce digitized retail, this sector remains a digital desert.

  • Revenue Leakage: Business owners lose 30–40% of potential revenue to missed orders, pricing disputes, and forgotten pickups — all tracked in tattered notebooks or WhatsApp chats.
  • Cash Flow Anxiety: Owners collect payment after service, leaving them unable to buy chemicals in bulk, hire help, or plan beyond today.
  • Zero Customer Trust: Customers have no visibility on pricing, order status, or delivery timelines. They switch providers monthly.
  • No Software Innovation: Unlike food (Chowdeck), mobility (Bolt), or beauty, laundry and bespoke fashion have seen virtually zero vertical software investment.
"Every morning, my aunt opened a tattered notebook. By evening, three customers had argued over prices she forgot she quoted. She never knew how much she would earn tomorrow." — Real operator in Lagos

The Solution: Stitch

Stitch is the first vertical SaaS platform purpose-built for Africa's laundry and fashion SMEs. Think of it as the "Shopify for Drycleaners" — with embedded fintech.

🏪 For Business Owners (B2B SaaS)

  • Digital order intake & garment-level tracking
  • Dynamic pricing by fabric, service & delivery zone
  • Staff task queues & driver logistics
  • Subscription engine: customers prepay for monthly "laundry units"
  • Customer CRM, loyalty & automated notifications

📱 For Customers (B2C Experience)

  • Mobile-first scheduling & live order tracking
  • Volume plan subscriptions (e.g., "50 shirts/month")
  • Transparent pricing — no more haggling
  • Seamless in-app payments via OPay
  • Real-time driver location & delivery ETAs
The game-changer is the subscription engine. For the first time, a drycleaner in Oshodi knows her revenue before the month begins. She can hire help. She can breathe.
